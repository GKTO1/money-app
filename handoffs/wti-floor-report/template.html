Oil market note · 8 October 2026 · WTI, next 3 months

If the straits stay shut, WTI’s floor is about $78

With the war on, Hormuz under attack and Bab al-Mandeb closed to Saudi ships, I expect NYMEX WTI to hold roughly $78 on weekly closes through early January, with about 80% confidence. The next dip most likely bottoms near $83. If both straits close to all traffic, the floor jumps to about $105.

How the floor is set

Four separate reads land between $78 and $83

The lows keep rising Market

Since the June deal collapsed, each sell-off on peace hopes has stopped higher. WTI fell roughly to the mid-$70s on early-August deal bets, to about $82 in late August, and to $88.28 on Oct 7 after the IEA sped up its release. Brent shows the same steps: $79.36, $87.84, $96.16. The early-August low needed active deal bets, and $78 sits just above it.

The cushion is thinner Barrels

The world was still about 5 mb/d short of pre-war supply in September, and early October looks worse. Emergency oil now arrives at 0.6–0.85 mb/d, down from 2.2 mb/d in the spring. Global stocks are 507 million barrels lower than in February, and the IEA expects lost demand to shrink from 5.3 mb/d in Q2 to 2.0 mb/d in Q4. The slack that let prices sag in summer is mostly gone.

The curve already prices some peace Futures

On Oct 5 the December contract (CLZ6) settled at $87.96 and January (CLF7) at $86.85. June 2027 traded near $82 and December 2027 near $76. Those prices blend in some chance of a deal. Without one, the nearby contracts should hold up, and the $76–82 back end shows where WTI heads only once Hormuz reopens.

Volatility sets the air pockets Model

Implied volatility (OVX) was 48.6 on Oct 7 and realized volatility has run about 50%, so drops of 10–15% inside two weeks happen about once a month. A 40,000-path simulation calibrated to that, pulling back toward a no-deal value near $93, puts the median three-month low at $83 and the 80% weekly floor at $78.

Supply

What’s missing, and what’s filling the hole

A closed strait has not meant an empty one. Iran declared Hormuz shut, yet in September about 10 mb/d of crude still crossed on tankers running dark or under US escort, roughly three-quarters of the pre-war 13.5 mb/d. That flow is the base case’s biggest variable, and it is shrinking: a record 10 tankers were hit between Sep 28 and Oct 4, only 7 commodity ships crossed on Oct 6, and Hormuz crude fell 27% in a week.

  • Emergency stocks. 325 million of the IEA’s 400-million-barrel March release has been delivered. The remaining 75–100 million barrels, which is most of what the G7’s “up to 100 million” refers to, works out to 0.6–0.85 mb/d into February. The US reserve holds 283 million barrels, its lowest since 1982.
  • Demand. The IEA puts 2026 demand 2.5 mb/d below 2025, about 3.35 mb/d less than it expected before the war. The year-on-year loss narrows to 2.0 mb/d in Q4 as consumers adjust.
  • Other producers. US output is heading for a record 13.9 mb/d and Brazil is up 18% on the year. Drone strikes have cut Russian and Kazakh supply, and OPEC+ has no meaningful spare capacity outside the Gulf.
  • Inventories. Global observed stocks are down 507 million barrels since February. US distillate stocks hit a 40-year low on Sep 11. Cushing holds about 24.7 million barrels and has built for two weeks, one reason WTI trades at a wide discount.

Aramco East-West pipeline

The damage, the repair time, and what it does to the price

The East-West line carries Saudi crude about 1,200 km from the eastern fields to Yanbu on the Red Sea, the kingdom’s only route around Hormuz. It reached its full 7 mb/d in March. Before September’s attack it ran about 5.5 mb/d, and about 4.5 mb/d of that was exported from Yanbu.

What happened Sep 10 – Oct 6

  • Drone waves on Sep 10–11 hit pumping stations. Reuters’ sources say stations 8 and 9; Argus says 3 of 11, one badly, with the pipe itself intact. A temporary bypass now routes around the worst-hit station.
  • Flow was zero from Sep 11 to 22. It restarted at 2–2.65 mb/d, reached 5.5 mb/d by Oct 1 and 5.8 mb/d on Oct 6, according to the energy minister.
  • New hits since: a projectile started a fire at Yanbu port on Oct 1 and briefly stopped loading. On Oct 4 the Houthis claimed a strike on Khurais. One AFP source says a pump station there was badly damaged, while Bloomberg’s sources and the minister’s figure point to normal flow. This is unresolved.

How long repairs take Estimates

  • Estimates range from 3–5 weeks (AP) and 4–6 weeks (Kpler) to “days” (US Energy Secretary Wright).
  • Precedents are fast. After the May 2019 drone strike on stations 8 and 9, pumping resumed the next day. After the 2019 Abqaiq attack, Saudi capacity was back in about 11 days.
  • With 5.8 of 7 mb/d already flowing, the remaining work is worth about 1.2 mb/d. The outlet is the tighter limit. Yanbu tops out near 4–4.5 mb/d of exports, and with Bab al-Mandeb closed to Saudi ships those barrels must go north through Suez and the 2.5 mb/d SUMED pipeline, or around Africa: 54 days to Korea instead of 24.

Why the price effect has been modest so far. Saudi Arabia has a relief valve. During the September outage it sent dark supertankers through Hormuz and lifted exports to as much as 6.6 mb/d. Brent rose about $7.50 and gave it all back within two weeks. In a full closure that valve shuts, the pipeline becomes the only Saudi outlet, and the same outage would be worth $20–30.

The EU diesel release

Big for diesel, small for WTI, and mostly barrels already promised

  • What was agreed. On Oct 2 the G7 backed releasing “up to” 100 million barrels over about four months, with a large diesel tranche within 20 days, around Oct 22. On Oct 7 IEA members agreed to deliver the rest of March’s pledges faster, diesel first. Since 325 million of the 400 million March barrels are already out, most of the G7 figure is those remaining barrels, not new oil. EU leaders meet Oct 15–16 and the IEA board reviews next week. Members still hold about 1.1 billion barrels, over 200 million of it diesel.
  • Scale. Roughly 0.6–0.85 mb/d into February, compared with 2.2 mb/d in March–July. Fifty million barrels of diesel is about a sixth of the EU’s emergency diesel and gasoil stocks.
  • Diesel market. The ICE gasoil crack fell from above $90 in September to about $73 on Oct 7 as the release came into view. US distillate stocks are about 12% below normal, and US diesel averaged $6.32 a gallon on Oct 6.
  • WTI. The effect is small and largely priced: WTI fell $1.16 on Oct 7 on the faster-release news. The release also ended Washington’s threat of a US diesel export ban (dropped Oct 2), which had put up to 2 mb/d of US refining runs at risk and widened WTI’s discount. I put it at $1–3 off the floor through December. When deliveries end around February, the floor rises if the straits are still shut.

Stress case

If both straits close to everyone, the floor is about $105

  • What stops. About 10 mb/d of Hormuz crude and 1 mb/d of products. Saudi west-coast exports fall to 2.5–3 mb/d, all heading north through Suez. About 280 Red Sea transits a week reroute around Africa, tying up tankers and lifting freight. The net loss jumps to 15–18 mb/d, three times today’s.
  • Smaller shock absorbers than in spring. The IEA could restart releases at the 2.2 mb/d pace it managed in March–July from its 1.1-billion-barrel pool, but the US reserve is down to 283 million barrels. About 3 mb/d of the demand lost in Q2 has come back and would have to be priced out again.
  • Precedent. From March to May, with Hormuz crude at 2.7–4.9 mb/d and Bab al-Mandeb still open, Brent averaged about $102 and WTI settled as high as $112.95. WTI touched $119.48 intraday on Mar 9 and Brent $126.41 on Apr 30. Goldman adds about $25 for a Bab al-Mandeb and Suez disruption on top of its $120-plus upside case, and Bank of America flags a $150-plus tail.
  • Floor. About $105 WTI, or $117 Brent at today’s spread. The first spike would likely run to $120–150.
  • The WTI-specific catch. With Brent at $140, pressure on Washington to limit US crude exports would be intense. Officials ruled out crude export curbs in August, but any curb would cap WTI and blow out the spread. That is the main way a long WTI position loses in a full closure.

Catalysts

What breaks the floor, and what lifts it

Breaks it Downside

  • A real US–Iran deal. This scenario rules it out, but it is the biggest risk. June’s memorandum took WTI to $68.58, and the $76–82 back of the curve is where it would head.
  • Deal talk without a deal. Reports of talks helped knock $1.16 off on Oct 7, and early-August deal bets took WTI to roughly the mid-$70s.
  • A new IEA release beyond March’s barrels. The board reviews next week.
  • WTI-only pressure: more Cushing builds, the Brent–WTI spread widening past $14, or US export curbs.
  • Washington before the Nov 3 midterms: up to 40 million barrels of reserve exchanges in November and December, plus pressure on allies.

Lifts it Upside

  • More tanker attacks pushing Hormuz flows toward zero.
  • New strikes on Saudi infrastructure. The pipeline is still 1.2 mb/d below capacity.
  • US strikes on Iran. Reports say the White House is drafting options.
  • Emergency barrels running out around February, with the US reserve at a 44-year low.
  • Demand coming back. The IEA sees the year-on-year loss shrinking to 2.0 mb/d in Q4.
  • Light positioning. Hedge-fund net length is near the 10th percentile, so short covering can be sharp.
  • Hurricane Isaias, which shut about a quarter of Gulf of Mexico output ahead of landfall.

Method and sources

How this was put together

  • Brent daily closes come from Financial Modeling Prep. WTI levels are settlements reported by Newsquawk, MarketScreener and CNBC. The EIA and FRED sites were blocked from this research environment and the FMP plan does not include WTI, so the chart uses Brent and converts the WTI levels at a $12 spread.
  • Flows, stocks and forecasts come from wire and trade-press reports up to Oct 8, mostly read through search excerpts. Conflicting or single-source items are flagged in the text. OPEC’s report on Oct 13 and the IEA’s on Oct 14 may revise them.
  • The floors are conditional on the scenario. They are not a forecast that the scenario will happen.
